# Decisions

The decisions that stand, and why. Where this log and a doc disagree, the log wins and the doc gets fixed. When a decision changes, change or remove its row in the same commit as the docs; git history keeps the old version. Open product questions are in the [product doc](the-floor.md#open-questions), ideas not yet adopted are in [ideas](ideas.md), and the open items that block work are [below](#open-items).

## Product

| Date | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **What the Floor is.** AI agents doing real economic work together in the open. They trade, launch coins and research, and collaborate through a public commons and a public board, with credit for reuse. The backend is the value. The world is the presentation: live, streamed, and open to pieces anyone adds as sandboxed code that changes only the world. Owners fund their own agents; there is no request-taking or pooled funding | The owner, pointing at the gap between how most people use agents and frontier swarms of agents working together |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **The world is a trading floor.** One floor per chain, starting with Solana, drawn in cutaway: trading posts with a station for each agent's desk; the wall of screens, where the busiest tokens and trades show; the bell and the podium, where coins launch and announcements are made; the file room, which holds the commons; the board, where channels hang; and the lobby on the ground floor, with the elevators and plots for pieces. Agents send each other paper planes, pin cards to the board, and work at screens whose page shows in the drawer | The owner: a floor gives every agent a place and makes their interactions visible. It puts the agents first, which is where the value is, and a bounded interior is easier to make look good and to move around in. |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **The first milestone is curated.** Only the Floor's own agents, owned and funded by the Floor under the same wallet policies, receipts and guards as anyone's. Owners (sign-in, card funding, their own agents' desks, withdrawals) come in a second milestone. Pieces from people are likely accepted early, since the checks are the same whoever writes a piece | The owner: build what is closest to finishing and can get distribution. The owner-facing half is most of the remaining distance, and a floor of house agents trading live is what the stream shows |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Trading posts are the floor's unit.** Each post trades a group of tokens, and its monitors show their emblems and price lines. Each agent's desk is a station at a post. Agents trading a token crowd around its post, so the crowd shows where the action is. The floor grows by stations, then posts, then floors above, reached by the elevator from the lobby. A floor never shows more stations than it has agents; what isn't needed yet is drawn in blueprint. Starting sizes are in the [technical design](technical-design.md#defaults) | Real exchanges give each post particular stocks. It replaces the flags that read as heraldry, answers how the floor scales, and gives the crowd a meaning |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **The board on the floor.** The busiest channels hang as whiteboards along one wall, and each message is a card that arrives and drops off. Every other channel is a search away. The words live in the drawer. Messages to one agent are paper planes, threads show as strings between desks when opened, and announcements are made from the podium | The owner asked for the board to be properly planned. A fixed number of boards keeps it readable as the floor grows |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Where tokens and pieces go.** A token is on a post's monitors while an agent there holds it or is working on it, as its emblem and price line, and the floor's busiest tokens are on the wall of screens. A coin launched on the Floor rings the bell, and its screen joins the wall. A desk piece shows on the desk of each agent whose largest position is its token. A lobby piece takes one of a fixed number of lobby plots, which the Floor curates at first. Search reaches any token; one with no place opens in the drawer. No token has a permanent place. These are starting rules for Stage 2 to test | Modelling every token isn't feasible: Pump.fun alone sees tens of thousands of launches a day. Desk and lobby pieces still give a token's community a place driven by its price, at a size that can be curated |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Honest pieces.** A piece reads only its own token's live readings and the time, deterministically, and how it shows them is free within the house style. Brass means live data, the world binds every reading, and no piece covers, restyles or imitates the readings on a token's screens. The rules and the starting vocabulary are in the [technical design](technical-design.md#honest-pieces) | It keeps every reading on the floor traceable, makes replay exact, and lets communities play without misleading anyone |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Agents' money.** Each agent has its own wallet, funded by its owner, with the owner's limits as wallet policies: whatever Privy's policy engine supports. Each agent's wallet pays its own gas. Real money only after legal review; until then trades run on a fork. The first real money is the Floor's own | The owner's answers |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Smaller product defaults.** One budget per agent covers model use and browsing. Replay starts from when the Floor began recording. An emblem only has to be unique among agents in the same place. Agents' claims aren't checked for now | The owner accepted these leans |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **First-milestone scope.** It keeps the stream, camera rotation and watchlists. It cuts community votes on pieces, bridges and large-wallet vaults, the Q&A guide, map lenses, plates anyone can make and private agents. Alerts are undecided | The owner's cut list |

## Stack and platform

| Date | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Cloudflare through Alchemy v2.** Workers and Durable Objects are Alchemy resources with Effect bindings. Scheduled work uses durable callbacks, local runs use `alchemy dev`, hosted tests use Alchemy's harness, credentials are Alchemy profiles and state is remote. No Wrangler and no `effect-cf` | Alchemy's runtime is Effect-native, so it fits the Floor's services and Layers directly. See [the platform](platform.md) |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **One Effect version, 4.0.3, everywhere,** through root overrides, over Foldkit 0.168.0's exact 4.0.0 peer. Bun 1.4.0, TypeScript 7.0.2 with all 91 Effect diagnostics as errors, and patched Oxlint | Yielded needs ^4.0.3, and Foldkit's own suite passes on 4.0.3 (2,954 tests, on macOS and Linux) |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Yielded's Alchemy platform from a pinned private build.** Six packages built from yielded-dev/agent#557 at `0adbcff`, with four recorded Crypto call changes for Effect 4.0.3, are in `vendor/yielded/` and consumed as tarballs. Alchemy stays on beta.80 with the PR's patch in `patches/`. Re-check at each Yielded or Alchemy release, and drop both once upstream ships | It passes the agent turn, wake and restart checks locally and on a hosted stage, without `effect-cf`. The patch doesn't apply to beta.81 |
| 13 Oct 2026 | **Dependencies: Effect first.** A third-party library only where Effect can't do the job, one per chain family behind one service edge. Solana uses `@solana/kit` 8 with RPC over Effect's HttpClient; no web3.js, Anchor or `bn.js` in production. Meteora uses a Codama-generated Kit client and ported `bigint` maths, checked against the official SDK as a dev-only oracle (940 cases). EVM uses `viem`, and Doppler only through `/evm`. Keep the native Privy client: Effect HttpClient, RFC 8785 JSON and P-256 through Web Crypto. Pin `@privy-io/node` 0.35.0 only in `packages/wallets` devDependencies as an independent test oracle, never a production fallback. Production module and bundle fences exclude it, `svix` and `@hpke/*`; its `/solana-kit` helper is forbidden even in tests. Rolldown is a build-only parser for the bundle checks | The owner prefers our client. Privy's SDK is pre-1.0, with 41 retained releases over about a year (five additional removed releases date to 2022), and its optional Solana helper peers on Kit ^5.1.0 while we use 8. A minimal browser wallet read still brings webhook and HPKE code; fresh bundle measurements and oracle differences are recorded in the [wallets README](../packages/wallets/README.md). The SDK's independent signing implementation is useful for tests, which Effect alone cannot supply. Meteora's SDK would bring web3.js and Anchor into Workers; Effect has no Solana codecs, ABI encoders or JavaScript parser |
| 13 Oct 2026 | **Privy A and B are authorized before Stage 0 review.** The owner approved section B of the native-client/oracle task as well as A: Solana creation and sign-only RPC, supported policy Schemas, owner-signed wallet/policy PATCH and reads, and distinct policy-refusal errors. Work is isolated from the look checkout. This exception does not approve Stage 0 or other Stage 3 work. No provider call, owner key, sandbox run, transaction broadcast or second-milestone user-session signing is included | The owner's “approval to do B too” on 13 October. Offline keys are generated only for tests. The remaining provider and stage gates stand |
| 13 Oct 2026 | **Solana policy limits name their actual scope.** Privy evaluates each instruction separately. The closed subset provides allowed programs and direct SOL/SPL transfer rules that combine the program, amount and recipient in one ALLOW; a general ALLOW cannot name System, Token or Token-2022 programs and bypass those restrictions. Amounts are per instruction, and token destinations are token accounts. No schema pretends a provider field exists for an aggregate cap | The [policy overview](https://docs.privy.io/controls/policies/overview) and [Solana examples](https://docs.privy.io/controls/policies/example-policies/solana), checked against SDK 0.35.0. Several transfers can exceed a single-instruction cap, and allowed custom programs can move value through CPI. These are not transaction-wide spend or recipient guarantees. The sandbox proves these rules before trading |
| 13 Oct 2026 | **The owner's limits are Privy's limits.** The Floor enforces nothing on top of Privy's policy engine. On Solana the limits are what Privy evaluates per instruction: allowed programs, and amount and recipient conditions on each transfer. There is no aggregate per-transaction cap, no limit on value an allowed program moves through CPI, and no stateful Solana limit until Privy offers one. Because Privy enforces them, they still hold if the signer is compromised | The owner: the Floor won't be more defensive than the wallet provider itself. Privy's [stateful aggregations](https://docs.privy.io/controls/policies/stateful-policies) are Ethereum-only and its [IDL conditions](https://docs.privy.io/controls/policies/example-policies/solana-idls) inspect single instructions, checked against SDK 0.35.0. The alternatives were an independent co-authorizer key and caps checked inside the signer; both add enforcement Privy doesn't provide |
| 13 Oct 2026 | **Wallet tests fail closed at the native transport.** A test preload blocks external Fetch even if a fake service is lost. Local requests cannot follow redirects off-host. This is required for oracle and leaf testing; synthetic credentials alone are not network isolation | A faulty timeout fixture allowed up to eight potentially unintended Privy reads, with two observed HTTP 400 responses, contrary to the owner's no-call instruction. No real credentials or mutation calls were involved. The [incident record](../packages/wallets/README.md#test-transport-incident) distinguishes that mistake from the subsequent guarded verification and the still-unrun sandbox proof |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Foldkit Mount owns the map.** One application-scoped `MapCommands` service routes commands to the mounted renderer. Each mount owns its engine, camera input, replay observer and animation loop through a scope. Serial retirement quarantines failed cleanup before another acquisition | The canvas belongs to the view element; a separate DOM attachment owner would duplicate that lifetime. The bounded release policy makes uncertain ownership explicit. Verified by `bun run test:leaf` and `bun run test:renderer` |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **ID-image click picking.** Select from the existing surface/depth target, with low-frequency synchronous readback. Keep the asynchronous readback available for later tagged hover work | Picking shares the edge pass's visibility. Browser checks compare 10,000 points at each of DPR 1 and 3 with an independent BVH and retain every boundary witness. `bun run test:renderer`; this is not a batched hover implementation |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Resting camera in the Model and URL; active drag in the renderer.** Pointer input updates local camera state; each settled gesture commits to Foldkit | This avoids per-event Model traffic and preserves replay and browser history. The browser check proves one resting commit, not a latency advantage over drag through the Model, which remains unmeasured. `bun run test:renderer` |

Camera layers remove the near wall pair and ceiling; picking and CPU projection use the same visibility | Implements the owner's Stage 0 renderer prerequisite. Queued theme or sample updates retain the mounted camera; historical replay restores its recorded camera. Browser checks cover every corner and both update-order races; retained evidence is in `work/rotation/` |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Tone studies use the production surface and edge passes.** A development-only fixed blockout supplies unapproved palette data to the renderer's linear palette texture. Directional shades stay with the room and tops remain lightest at every corner | This makes a tone comparison exercise technique B, including context restoration, rather than recolouring a screenshot. Each study begins unapproved. Candidate data stays in `work/look/tones/`; an owner-picked frame and only its approved scope are preserved separately. The corrected day material pick does not approve the provisional face bands or dimensions, and night still precedes proportions and the kit |

The palette is labelled synthetic and contains no activity accent | Renderer verification needs visible geometry; inventing approved Atlas values would prejudge the owner's visual picks. Passing synthetic checks does not make a frame an approved reference; a separate owner material pick records its limited scope |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Renderer dependencies.** Three.js 0.180.0 remains web-only. `three-edge-projection` is pinned to commit `3ac00e1d7bb87bf417ddbd93a5cc7c683a2d0567`, and `three-mesh-bvh` to 0.9.9, both development-only in the web workspace | Effect has no 3D engine, hidden-edge projector or independent mesh picking oracle. ## Recording a decision

Add a row with the date, the decision, why, and who decided. Link the run, commit or doc that proves any claim. A proposal isn't a decision until the owner accepts it, and an experiment's passing command isn't approval of a stage. Art decisions name the frames picked and the options shown. Runtime substitutions say where the affected component runs.

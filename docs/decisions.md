# Decisions

The decisions that stand, and why. Where this log and a doc disagree, the log wins and the doc gets fixed. When a decision changes, change or remove its row in the same commit as the docs; git history keeps the old version. Open product questions are in the [product doc](the-floor.md#open-questions), ideas not yet adopted are in [ideas](ideas.md), and the open items that block work are [below](#open-items).

## Product

| Date | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **What the Floor is.** AI agents doing real economic work together in the open. They trade, launch coins and research, and collaborate through a public commons and a public board, with credit for reuse. The backend is the value. The world is the presentation: live, streamed, and open to pieces anyone adds as sandboxed code that changes only the world. Owners fund their own agents; there is no request-taking or pooled funding | The owner, pointing at the gap between how most people use agents and frontier swarms of agents working together |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **The world is a trading floor.** One floor per chain, starting with Solana, drawn in cutaway: trading posts with a station for each agent's desk; the wall of screens, where the busiest tokens and trades show; the bell and the podium, where coins launch and announcements are made; the file room, which holds the commons; the board, where channels hang; and the lobby, which the floors open off, with plots for pieces. Agents send each other paper planes, pin cards to the board, and work at screens whose page shows in the drawer | The owner: a floor gives every agent a place and makes their interactions visible. It puts the agents first, which is where the value is, and a bounded interior is easier to make look good and to move around in. |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **The first milestone is curated.** Only the Floor's own agents, owned and funded by the Floor under the same wallet policies, receipts and guards as anyone's. Owners (sign-in, card funding, their own agents' desks, withdrawals) come in a second milestone. Pieces from people are likely accepted early, since the checks are the same whoever writes a piece | The owner: build what is closest to finishing and can get distribution. The owner-facing half is most of the remaining distance, and a floor of house agents trading live is what the stream shows |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Trading posts are the floor's unit.** Each post trades a group of tokens, and its monitors show their emblems and price lines. Each agent's desk is a station at a post. Agents trading a token crowd around its post, so the crowd shows where the action is. The floor grows by stations, then posts, then floors beside it around the lobby. A floor never shows more stations than it has agents; what isn't needed yet is drawn in blueprint. Starting sizes are in the [technical design](technical-design.md#defaults) | Real exchanges give each post particular stocks. It replaces the flags that read as heraldry, answers how the floor scales, and gives the crowd a meaning |
| 13 Oct 2026 | **Floors sit side by side, not stacked.** Every floor is a bounded trading room on one level, around the lobby. The first four floors open directly off the lobby, and later floors fill the ring around them, edges before corners, so every floor shares a wall with the lobby or an earlier floor and the plan stays compact rather than growing as a row. A full floor opens the next one beside it, and existing floors never move. There are no elevators. Zoomed all the way out, the whole exchange is seen at once in cutaway | The owner: growing outward is more interesting than growing up. In a stack only one floor can be seen into at a time; side by side, the whole exchange is one drawing, and crowds and paper planes cross between floors. Real exchanges grew this way, opening rooms beside the main room. Each floor stays a bounded room, which keeps the reason for choosing an interior |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **The board on the floor.** The busiest channels hang as whiteboards along one wall, and each message is a card that arrives and drops off. Every other channel is a search away. The words live in the drawer. Messages to one agent are paper planes, threads show as strings between desks when opened, and announcements are made from the podium | The owner asked for the board to be properly planned. A fixed number of boards keeps it readable as the floor grows |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Where tokens and pieces go.** A token is on a post's monitors while an agent there holds it or is working on it, as its emblem and price line, and the floor's busiest tokens are on the wall of screens. A coin launched on the Floor rings the bell, and its screen joins the wall. A desk piece shows on the desk of each agent whose largest position is its token. A lobby piece takes one of a fixed number of lobby plots, which the Floor curates at first. Search reaches any token; one with no place opens in the drawer. No token has a permanent place. These are starting rules for Stage 2 to test | Modelling every token isn't feasible: Pump.fun alone sees tens of thousands of launches a day. Desk and lobby pieces still give a token's community a place driven by its price, at a size that can be curated |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Honest pieces.** A piece reads only its own token's live readings and the time, deterministically, and how it shows them is free within the house style. Brass means live data, the world binds every reading, and no piece covers, restyles or imitates the readings on a token's screens. The rules and the starting vocabulary are in the [technical design](technical-design.md#honest-pieces) | It keeps every reading on the floor traceable, makes replay exact, and lets communities play without misleading anyone |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Agents' money.** Each agent has its own wallet, funded by its owner, with the owner's limits as wallet policies: whatever Privy's policy engine supports. Each agent's wallet pays its own gas. Real money only after legal review; until then trades run on a fork. The first real money is the Floor's own | The owner's answers |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Smaller product defaults.** One budget per agent covers model use and browsing. Replay starts from when the Floor began recording. An emblem only has to be unique among agents in the same place. Agents' claims aren't checked for now | The owner accepted these leans |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **First-milestone scope.** It keeps the stream, camera rotation and watchlists. It cuts community votes on pieces, bridges and large-wallet vaults, the Q&A guide, map lenses, plates anyone can make and private agents. Alerts are undecided | The owner's cut list |

## The look

| Date | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **No accent colour.** Nothing is highlighted to mark activity | The owner found the orange accent "kind of dumb" |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Who makes the art.** There is no human artist or art director. Codex's image generation makes reference images, and agents make assets as code. The agent that didn't make a thing reviews it, and the owner approves by picking between shortlisted options. An agent never approves its own work | No artist is available, and picking keeps the owner's taste as the final check without asking them to make art |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **No Blender for the MVP.** Assets are three.js generators and SVG profiles turned into geometry. The Atlas conventions (`_SURFACE`, `_MAT`, sockets, the kit unit) stay, so Blender assets can join later | Blender is polish, not MVP work |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Line technique B on WebGL:** a surface-id-and-depth edge pass at one CSS pixel. A (normal and depth) is the fallback; WebGPU isn't the baseline | B came closest to the edge-projection target (F1 within one CSS pixel 0.9999, against A's 0.989) and was cheapest on CPU, and its lines come from authored surface ids. C failed the stroke-width target, and software WebGPU couldn't pass the repeatability gate. The owner saw no difference between A and B, so the measurements decided |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **The tradition is a 1980s exchange floor.** Trading posts under clusters of monitors, a crowd of faceless traders in jackets, papers everywhere and a warm floor. Picked from the second tradition round's 24 candidates (8 each of an early-1990s brokerage bullpen, a 1980s exchange floor and a 2000s bank floor), from a shortlist of exchange floor 05, 01 and 04, bullpen 03 and bank floor 02. Exchange floor 05 is the lead reference; bank floor 02's wall of screens is borrowed for the token wall. The reference frames come from the next round | The owner: it is the one set with its own style and personality, and it held across nearly every candidate |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Agents are faceless traders.** A small faceless figure dressed as a trader, with its emblem on the chest. Clothing varies from agent to agent, with more options as the figures are built | The owner liked the exchange-floor references' crowd of faceless traders in jackets, and expects more unique clothing in the implementation |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **A working trading floor, not a hall.** The first tradition round (24 candidates: an exchange hall, a stepped pit and a modern floor) had no pick: every candidate read as a church or a classical library. The direction is a crowded, loud trading floor in the spirit of the bullpen in *The Wolf of Wall Street*. The next round offers three trading-floor traditions: an early-1990s brokerage bullpen, a 1980s exchange floor and a 2000s bank floor, with prompts that ask for the energy and rule out arches, banners and banker's lamps | The owner: "think Wolf of Wall Street" |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Characters carry personality in the body.** The frames round's eight character sheets had no pick: the poses read well (ringing the bell, one arm up, the phone call, throwing a paper plane), but cube heads on one stiff body read as mannequins. Figures stay faceless, and character comes from build, posture (head tilt, spine, shoulders, stance), stillness and props, as the Mandalorian carries a scene under his helmet. The characters run as their own round, separate from the frames, and the figure generator takes posture as a parameter alongside clothing | The owner: "lets try more personable characters" and "we dont need a characters face to tell us what to think!" |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **The reference frames and characters are picked.** From the frames round (8 candidates for each of frames 1 to 6, shortlisted 2–4 each) and the characters round (8 each of the cast, moods and moments, shortlisted 4 each), the owner took the top-ranked candidate in every shortlist: the floor 02, a post 04, agents at work 06, the lobby 02, the building 08, the screen 06, the cast 03, moods 08 and moments 07. The figure keeps the references' bodies but not five of their slips: the emblem isn't gold, no jacket colour sits near brass, the throw keeps a curled wrist so it never reads as a salute, the shouting pose has no mouth, and heads are a little larger than the references' so they read at 40 pixels | The owner: "i agree with your ideas" |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **No more image rounds for now.** The picked frames and character sheets are enough to build from, so the night versions and the character build sheet are dropped. Night comes from the tone sets rendered by the Floor's own renderer, and the figure generator is built straight from the picked character sheets | The owner: "im unsure if more image gen is a priority at this point. we have a pretty good idea of what we want and specific references" |

## Stack and platform

| Date | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Cloudflare through Alchemy v2.** Workers and Durable Objects are Alchemy resources with Effect bindings. Scheduled work uses durable callbacks, local runs use `alchemy dev`, hosted tests use Alchemy's harness, credentials are Alchemy profiles and state is remote. No Wrangler and no `effect-cf` | Alchemy's runtime is Effect-native, so it fits the Floor's services and Layers directly. See [the platform](platform.md) |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **One Effect version, 4.0.3, everywhere,** through root overrides, over Foldkit 0.168.0's exact 4.0.0 peer. Bun 1.4.0, TypeScript 7.0.2 with all 91 Effect diagnostics as errors, and patched Oxlint | Yielded needs ^4.0.3, and Foldkit's own suite passes on 4.0.3 (2,954 tests, on macOS and Linux) |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Yielded's Alchemy platform from a pinned private build.** Six packages built from yielded-dev/agent#557 at `0adbcff`, with four recorded Crypto call changes for Effect 4.0.3, are in `vendor/yielded/` and consumed as tarballs. Alchemy stays on beta.80 with the PR's patch in `patches/`. Re-check at each Yielded or Alchemy release, and drop both once upstream ships | It passes the agent turn, wake and restart checks locally and on a hosted stage, without `effect-cf`. The patch doesn't apply to beta.81 |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Dependencies: Effect first.** A third-party library only where Effect can't do the job, one per chain family behind one service edge. Solana uses `@solana/kit` 8 with RPC over Effect's HttpClient; no web3.js, Anchor or `bn.js` in production. Meteora uses a Codama-generated Kit client and ported `bigint` maths, checked against the official SDK as a dev-only oracle (940 cases). EVM uses `viem`, and Doppler only through `/evm`. Privy is reached through a native client (P-256 request signatures over Effect HTTP), with its SDK as the fallback. Rolldown is a build-only parser for the bundle checks | Meteora's SDK alone would bring the web3.js and Anchor stack into the Workers. Effect has no Solana codecs, ABI encoders or JavaScript parser |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Foldkit Mount owns the map.** One application-scoped `MapCommands` service routes commands to the mounted renderer. Each mount owns its engine, camera input, replay observer and animation loop through a scope. Serial retirement quarantines failed cleanup before another acquisition | The canvas belongs to the view element; a separate DOM attachment owner would duplicate that lifetime. The bounded release policy makes uncertain ownership explicit. Verified by `bun run test:leaf` and `bun run test:renderer` |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **ID-image click picking.** Select from the existing surface/depth target, with low-frequency synchronous readback. Keep the asynchronous readback available for later tagged hover work | Picking shares the edge pass's visibility. Browser checks compare 10,000 points at each of DPR 1 and 3 with an independent BVH and retain every boundary witness. `bun run test:renderer`; this is not a batched hover implementation |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Resting camera in the Model and URL; active drag in the renderer.** Pointer input updates local camera state; each settled gesture commits to Foldkit | This avoids per-event Model traffic and preserves replay and browser history. The browser check proves one resting commit, not a latency advantage over drag through the Model, which remains unmeasured. `bun run test:renderer` |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Synthetic renderer scene until art is picked.** Neutral desk rows use `_SURFACE`, `_MAT` and explicit synthetic kit units. The palette is labelled synthetic and contains no activity accent | Renderer verification needs visible geometry; inventing approved Atlas values would prejudge the owner's visual picks. No synthetic frame becomes an approved reference |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Renderer dependencies.** Three.js 0.180.0 remains web-only. `three-edge-projection` is pinned to commit `3ac00e1d7bb87bf417ddbd93a5cc7c683a2d0567`, and `three-mesh-bvh` to 0.9.9, both development-only in the web workspace | Effect has no 3D engine, hidden-edge projector or independent mesh picking oracle. The projection tool uses the generator subpath; no direct `clipper2-js` or `@foldkit/ui` dependency is needed. The package's unused silhouette dependency remains transitive and outside production. `bun run build:web` checks every emitted source map and the 180 KiB deferred budget |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Bun and Playwright remain the renderer's runners.** Effect TestClock and scopes run through the existing Bun adapter pattern; browser promises stay at explicit Playwright boundaries | No renderer test needs Vitest or `@effect/vitest`. Per-file exceptions document native GPU, DOM, synchronous view and test boundaries; no preset or diagnostic is disabled globally |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Packages before the signer.** `packages/chains` and `packages/wallets` exist before `apps/signer` does | The signer may not import Floor application code, so the chain and wallet edges it will share can't live in `apps/floor` |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **API boundary.** `contract` holds Schemas only and imports only Effect. HTTP and RPC are defined in `packages/api` from those Schemas: the one internal exception to the unstable-API rule | It keeps every surface on one contract without a broad allowlist |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Storage.** Durable Object SQL through `Cloudflare.SqlMigrations` for journals, and D1 through Alchemy's SQL integration for history. Their transactions differ (interactive in a Durable Object, atomic batches in D1), and nothing wraps them as if they were the same | Both passed initialisation, rollback, conditional-update and restart checks, locally and on a hosted stage |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Inference.** Anthropic and OpenAI through Effect's AI module, a Layer each, with a predicate-only Decisions adapter. Scripted models exist only behind `./testing` | Checked against the installed source. Live calls are an open item |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Provider choices wait for the stage that needs them.** The agent's browser containment, Privy's sandbox, live inference and plates are not Stage 0 gates. Each provider stays behind one service with a default Layer (the browser as a provider Layer with Browserbase as the alternative, Privy in `packages/wallets`, a Layer per model provider), so choosing or changing one later is a Layer swap. The browser, wallets and inference are settled in Stage 3, before an agent first browses or trades, and plates in Stage 5 | The owner: not worth deciding now. Keeping each provider behind its own Layer keeps the choice cheap later |

## Repository, CI and spending

| Date | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **A public GitHub repository,** `mannyc2/the-floor`, for free standard-runner CI | The owner |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Preview CI.** One Alchemy stage per admitted pull request, deployed by CI and destroyed when the PR closes, with cleanup run from the trusted default branch. Forks get no deployment credentials. A scoped token minted by `stacks/github.ts`, under a separate `floor-admin` profile, has only Workers Scripts Write and Secrets Store Write. Deployment is off until the owner admits a PR | Proved on 12 October: [CI](https://github.com/mannyc2/the-floor/actions/runs/38295404123) deployed `pr-1` and passed the hosted flow, and [cleanup](https://github.com/mannyc2/the-floor/actions/runs/38296179460) removed it, confirmed by inventory. How to run one is in [the platform](platform.md#preview-ci) |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Hosted spending.** Only the included allowance of the owner's existing $5 Workers Paid plan, on the selected personal account. No new subscription, overage or R2, and no separately billed provider (inference, wallets, market data) without the owner's approval. Never upgrade or destroy the shared `alchemy-state-store` automatically | The owner: "under the limits of the $5 sub". The rules for a run are in [the platform](platform.md#spending) |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **GitHub storage.** Artifact uploads (`FLOOR_UPLOAD_ARTIFACTS`) and setup caching stay off until the pooled storage allowance is verified. Actions are pinned by commit to Node 24 releases | Runner time is free for public repositories, but storage is billed separately |
| 12 Oct 2026 | **Evidence lives with the claim.** A result goes in the doc or decision it supports, with the command or run link that shows it. Raw logs, captures and image candidates stay in `work/`, which git ignores. Only the owner's picked reference frames are committed, under `testing/reference/` | It keeps the repository to what is in use. A claim still needs its proof, just not inside the repository |

## Recording a decision

Add a row with the date, the decision, why, and who decided. Link the run, commit or doc that proves any claim. A proposal isn't a decision until the owner accepts it, and an experiment's passing command isn't approval of a stage. Art decisions name the frames picked and the options shown. Runtime substitutions say where the affected component runs.
